# Writing industry materials for an economics PhD

Distilled from the knowledge base's `15-market-wisdom.md` (sources there) and dayuan-wang/job-hunt-helper.

## Resume (one page)

- Objective, two lines: who you are for this role + the 2–3 methods the posting names that you
  really have. E.g. "Economics Ph.D. (May 2027) who builds causal and forecasting models on
  large panel data; DiD / event study, structural estimation, Python and SQL."
- Projects as business problems: question → data and size → method → result as a number →
  what a decision-maker would do with it. Plain words first, technical name second.
- Skills: only `06-skills.md` words; mirror the posting's vocabulary where it is the same skill
  ("A/B testing" vs "experimentation"); nothing from the do-not-claim list.
- Cut: paper abstracts, full publication lists, coursework unless asked, references.
- Teaching becomes communication: "explained pricing and demand analysis to 120 business students".

## Cover letter (350–450 words, five parts)

1. Opener (kb): the role, your PhD and date, one-line positioning.
2. Depth: one flagship project that matches the job's core problem.
3. Breadth: one more experience that matches a second requirement.
4. Why them: something specific from their team page, product, cases or research.
5. Closer (kb).
No em or en dashes, no generic praise, no "I am passionate about".

## By role type

- **Tech economist / DS**: causal inference (DiD, IV, RD), experimentation design, forecasting;
  SQL and Python are often tested; behavioral questions (Amazon: Leadership Principles).
- **Economic consulting**: communication, teamwork, quick learning; Excel and Stata; antitrust,
  damages, demand estimation, event studies; often a JOE posting with letters.
- **Finance / economic research**: macro views, time series, factor models; probability puzzles
  for quant roles.
