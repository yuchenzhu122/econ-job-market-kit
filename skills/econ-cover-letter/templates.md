# Two cover letter templates

Distilled from economics job market guides and university career centers (sources at the end).
The letter is built from the candidate's own paragraphs in `config.json`; these templates decide
the order, length, and what the per-school paragraph must do. They never supply facts.

## A. Research-oriented (research universities, policy schools, central banks)

Economics norm: one page, plain, research first. Many committees read it in under a minute;
most letters need little tailoring (Cawley's job market guide: state the job, your fields and
JMP topic, and that you would welcome an interview), with real tailoring only for close matches.

| # | Paragraph | Content |
|---|-----------|---------|
| 1 | `intro` | Position applied for, Ph.D. program and completion date, fields. |
| 2 | `jmp` | Question, data and method, main findings, why it matters. |
| 3 | `other_research` | One or two other papers, coauthors named, one result each. |
| 4 | `teaching` | Short credibility signal: instructor of record, evaluations, courses you can teach. |
| 5 | fit | 2 to 3 sentences (under ~60 words, to stay on one page) built from the requirement map (SKILL.md step 2a): the strongest real match first (a field the ad names or "particularly" wants, a group or center on the department site), then the duties or criteria the ad stresses that the other paragraphs do not cover. Skip a point rather than write a generic sentence. |
| 6 | `closing` | Enclosures, letter writers, thanks. |

## B. Teaching-focused (liberal arts colleges, regional universities, teaching tracks, community colleges)

Committees here read the letter closely and look for evidence of teaching and of interest in
*their* institution (Holmes and Colander on liberal arts college hiring; UNC Writing Center;
Binghamton career center; Inside Higher Ed). Teaching comes first and takes the most space;
research is condensed and tied to students. Up to two pages is normal.

| # | Paragraph | Content |
|---|-----------|---------|
| 1 | `intro_teaching` | Position, degree and date, and that teaching is central to the career you want; one line on research fields. |
| 2 | `teaching_approach` | How you teach, with concrete classroom examples (not a philosophy in the abstract). |
| 3 | `teaching_evidence` | Evaluations and what you changed in response; help outside class; relevant experience with students. |
| 4 | `research_brief` | Research in a few sentences, plainly worded, connected to students and teaching. |
| 5 | fit | The longest tailored part (3 to 6 sentences): named courses from *their* catalog or ad that you can teach, their student population or mission as the school describes it, teaching load or format named in the ad (e.g. online, large intro sections), and why this kind of institution. |
| 6 | `closing_teaching` | Enclosures (teaching statement, evaluations), letter writers, thanks. |

## Rules for the fit paragraph (both templates)

- Facts about the school come only from the ad and the school's own pages; facts about the
  candidate come only from `config.json`, the CV, and the statements. Never invent courses taught,
  mentoring, service, awards, collaborations, or ties to faculty.
- Name specific things (a course title, a center, the load, a program) instead of praise
  ("prestigious", "vibrant"). One faculty name at most, only as shared interest, only from the
  department's site.
- Read the whole job description (including the employer's HR page and position
  description) and build the requirement map before writing; every sentence in the fit
  paragraph should answer a specific line of the ad.
- Use the ad's own key words where they are true of the candidate (e.g. "applied learning",
  "large-enrollment courses", "data analytics"), without copying whole phrases.
- If the ad asks the letter to address something (inclusive teaching, a specific field, start
  date, degree timing), answer it in the fit paragraph using only true statements; if it cannot
  be answered truthfully, tell the user.
- Avoid: "I am excited to", "ideal candidate", "not only ... but also", em dashes, long
  clause-stacked sentences.

## Sources

- J. Cawley, *A Guide and Advice for Economists on the U.S. Junior Academic Job Market* (AEA/JOE), as quoted in Iowa State economics, "Preparing Materials for the Job Market" (2015).
- J. Holmes and D. Colander, on what liberal arts colleges look for when hiring economists (Middlebury experience), as cited in the same guide.
- UNC Writing Center, "Academic Cover Letters": research-first vs teaching-first structures.
- Binghamton University Career Center, "Cover Letters (for Faculty roles)": teaching-intensive vs research-intensive structures.
- K. Kelsky, "The Professor Is In: Research First or Teaching?", The Chronicle of Higher Education; Inside Higher Ed advice essay on academic cover letters (2013).
- UC Irvine Economics sample cover letter (teaching-oriented application to a regional university).
